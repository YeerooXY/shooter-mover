# Progression, strongboxes and long-term loot

## Status

Product-planning document only. Exact experience curves, odds and tier tables are not final.

## Confirmed direction

- Levels 1–100 should progress quickly at first and increasingly slowly later.
- Releasing first with a cap around level 65, then expanding the cap in later updates, is a preferred strategy.
- Players should feel satisfied with strongbox results without relying on reroll exploits.
- Exact item instances, not abstract definitions, are the durable reward.
- Four weapons is the maximum equipped weapon count in the current character model.
- Strongboxes should remain exciting at endgame without making ordinary mission rewards worthless.

## Progression goals

The progression curve should support three phases:

### Early game

- frequent level-ups;
- rapid access to the Hub systems;
- visible introduction of weapon families;
- low-cost experimentation;
- no long grind before the game becomes interesting.

### Mid game

- class skills begin defining builds;
- crafting and augments become meaningful;
- higher marks and rarities enter the loot pool;
- difficulty choices start changing encounters and rewards;
- level-ups remain regular but no longer occur every few minutes.

### Endgame

- progression shifts from constant level-ups to build refinement;
- rare weapon families, augments, overclocks and mode milestones become goals;
- the game provides repeatable challenges, not only a longer XP bar;
- cap expansions add new content without deleting the value of previous equipment.

## Working release-cap structure

### Initial release: cap near 65

A level-65 cap provides room for:

- a complete early and mid-game campaign arc;
- meaningful class skill development;
- MK1 and MK2 weapons plus selected MK3/endgame previews;
- several strongbox tiers;
- an initial endless mode and difficulty ladder;
- a real endgame before the full level-100 progression is ready.

### Later expansions: 65 toward 100

Cap increases should arrive with:

- new levels and enemies;
- new weapon marks or families;
- new skill branches;
- higher difficulty/mode milestones;
- new strongbox and crafting targets;
- catch-up improvements for new characters.

An expansion should not simply increase enemy health and make all existing gear obsolete.

## Strongbox philosophy

A strongbox should answer three player questions clearly:

1. What tier did I earn?
2. What exact item did it contain?
3. What useful choice do I have if I do not want the item?

The answer to the third question is essential. Keep, Sell and later Dismantle ensure an unwanted reward still advances the player.

## Anti-reroll rules

- Reward generation commits before reveal or at one authoritative opening step.
- Closing, restarting or reconnecting cannot reroll the exact outcome.
- Repeated opening requests return the same result or an exact duplicate/no-change response.
- A conflicting operation cannot create a second reward.
- The game should not be balanced around players exploiting save rollback or box rerolls.
- Bad-luck protection and useful disposition are healthier than hidden reroll opportunities.

## Strongbox tier memory

Several high-tier visual concepts have already been explored:

- Tier 8 — Emerald;
- Tier 10 — Red Diamond;
- Tier 11 — Antimatter containment;
- Tier 12 — dark “Glitch” / question-mark concept.

These names and art directions are preserved as concept memory. The final tier ordering, exact materials and reward tables remain open.

## Working tier behaviour

A box tier may influence:

- rarity weighting;
- eligible special definitions;
- augment-capacity likelihood;
- item-level metadata;
- chance of a signature or top-tier reward;
- quantity of fallback money/scrap rewards where appropriate.

A tier should not rewrite the permanent identity of a selected weapon family after selection.

## Satisfaction without guaranteed perfection

The goal is not for every box to contain the player's best possible item. The goal is for every box to feel useful.

Possible tools:

- duplicate-aware weighting without hard exclusion;
- visible bad-luck counters for rare milestones;
- first-discovery weighting for unseen families;
- guaranteed progress currency at defined intervals;
- strong Sell/Dismantle value for unwanted high-tier items;
- collection or mastery credit;
- deterministic milestone boxes after major clears;
- targetable boxes tied to weapon category, damage channel or mode.

## Working reward ladder

| Source | Reward character |
|---|---|
| normal enemy | low direct drop chance or run-local progress |
| elite enemy | improved box chance or guaranteed progress material |
| boss | guaranteed box/reward package and milestone credit |
| mission completion | XP, money and collected run rewards |
| difficulty first clear | deterministic special reward |
| endless milestone | box/material at fixed wave or room thresholds |
| raid completion | participant reward package and personal exact loot |

## Item-level and authored statistics

Current architectural direction treats weapon combat statistics as authored definition values. Item level is progression and presentation metadata rather than a universal multiplier for damage, cadence, projectile speed or other firing behaviour.

Future progression should preserve this clarity. Power can come from:

- stronger marks or definitions;
- rarity-specific authored mechanics;
- exact augments;
- overclocks;
- class skills;
- armour and build synergy;
- better player execution.

Avoid a system where two visually identical weapons differ only because one has a much larger hidden level multiplier.

## Retention after reaching the cap

Cap-level players need several parallel goals:

- complete weapon-family collection;
- find better exact instances and augment combinations;
- finish class skill/mastery goals;
- clear higher difficulties;
- progress endless-mode milestones and leaderboards;
- earn overclock cores and signature upgrades;
- complete raids or boss challenges;
- prepare for future cap/content expansions.

Not every goal should be an infinite numerical grind.

## Fairness principles

- A player should understand why a reward was eligible.
- Time investment should produce visible progress even during bad luck.
- High-end weapon costs should correspond to reasonable hours of play.
- New cap expansions should preserve meaningful value in old collections.
- Competitive multiplayer, if added, may need separate power-normalised rules.
- Real-money spending should never be required to reroll or obtain combat viability.

## Open decisions

- Final launch cap and exact expansion schedule.
- XP curve and expected hours per progression band.
- Number and naming of strongbox tiers.
- Exact pity/bad-luck system.
- Whether boxes target categories or remain broad loot pools.
- Whether unopened boxes are character-bound or account-stored.
- Whether collections and masteries are character-local or account-wide.
- How much old gear remains viable after a cap expansion.
- Whether the Glitch tier is a true highest tier, a special event tier or a separate mystery category.
